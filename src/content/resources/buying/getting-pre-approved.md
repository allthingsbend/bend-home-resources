## What pre-approval should help you answer

A useful pre-approval conversation should clarify your likely loan type, price range, down-payment scenarios, estimated payment, cash-to-close and major underwriting issues that could affect the purchase.

Ask the lender to show multiple scenarios when you are deciding between putting more money down and keeping more cash available after closing.

## Documents to gather early

- Recent pay statements and employment information.
- W-2s or tax documentation when requested for your income type.
- Bank and investment statements used for assets or reserves.
- Documentation for gift funds, if applicable.
- Information about existing debts and recurring obligations.
- Identification and any additional documents your lender requests.

## Compare more than the interest rate

Two quotes with the same rate can have different lender fees, points, credits and assumptions. Compare the loan estimate line by line once you have formal estimates.

Ask how long a rate lock lasts, what happens if closing moves, whether there is a float-down option, and which fees are lender-controlled versus third-party.

## Verify the lender or loan originator

Oregon regulates mortgage lenders and loan originators. NMLS Consumer Access is one place consumers can use to check licensing information for nonbank mortgage professionals.

Local service can be valuable during a transaction, but 'local' should not replace comparing the actual loan economics and responsiveness.

## Avoid changes before closing

Large new debts, job changes, unexplained account transfers or moving money around without documenting it can create extra underwriting work. Ask your lender before making a financial move you are unsure about.

Continue saving updated statements and pay information because underwriting may request refreshed documents.
