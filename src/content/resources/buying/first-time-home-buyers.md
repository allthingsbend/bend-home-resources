## Start with the monthly number, not the listing price

A comfortable purchase price is the one that still works after the mortgage, property taxes, homeowners insurance, utilities, maintenance and the repairs that inevitably show up after closing. A lender can tell you what you may qualify for; your household still has to decide what feels sustainable.

Keep cash needs beyond the down payment in view. Depending on the transaction, buyers may also need funds for inspections, appraisal-related costs, closing costs, moving and early repairs.

## Build the right team before you are under pressure

In Oregon, buyers working with a broker generally use a written buyer representation agreement. Read the scope, term, compensation language and cancellation provisions before signing, and ask questions until you understand them.

A strong team may include a real estate broker, lender, home inspector and—when the house calls for it—specialists such as a sewer-scope provider, roofer, electrician, HVAC contractor, plumber or structural professional.

- Compare at least a few lenders or loan options before choosing.
- Choose an agent based on fit, local knowledge and communication—not just the first referral.
- Know how quickly your inspector and specialist vendors can schedule during a contingency period.

## Treat due diligence as a project

The general home inspection is the starting point, not a guarantee that every hidden condition is known. Pay attention to moisture, drainage, roof condition, electrical, plumbing, heating/cooling, structure, exterior envelope and anything the inspector recommends for specialist review.

Also research the property itself. Deschutes County DIAL can provide property information, tax and assessment data and other records; City of Bend permit resources can help with permit research for properties inside city limits.

## Understand Bend-specific questions

Bend homes can differ meaningfully by age, neighborhood, exposure and construction. Ask about wildfire considerations, drainage and grading, freeze protection, irrigation, roof and gutter condition, heating systems, HOA documents where applicable and the history of remodels or additions.

The City of Bend also has a Home Energy Score program for homes listed for sale in the city. Use the score as one more data point, not as a substitute for an inspection or utility-history questions.

## Plan the first year before closing

Make a simple three-column list: must do immediately, should do in the first year, and optional improvement. That keeps cosmetic projects from crowding out water management, safety, roofing, electrical or other higher-priority work.

Once you own the home, a seasonal maintenance routine matters more than a giant one-time checklist. Bend's freeze-thaw cycles, dry summers and wildfire season make timing important.
