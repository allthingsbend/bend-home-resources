## Separate your walk-away number from your opening number

Decide what the property is worth to you before negotiation pressure takes over. Your maximum should reflect the house's condition, expected repairs, financing and how the purchase affects the rest of your finances.

Comparable sales and market conditions matter, but there is still a personal decision: what would you be comfortable paying if the seller accepts immediately?

## Know the non-price terms

- Inspection and due-diligence timelines.
- Financing and appraisal-related terms.
- Closing and possession timing.
- Seller-paid credits or concessions.
- Personal property or fixtures included in the sale.
- Any property-specific documents or investigations you need.

## Protect time for real due diligence

Before shortening an inspection period to make an offer more competitive, make sure inspectors and specialists can actually complete the work you may need. A compressed timeline is only useful if you can execute it.

For homes with visible moisture concerns, older systems, roof questions, additions or other red flags, build your investigation plan before submitting the offer.

## Think ahead to repair negotiations

An inspection is not a shopping list of every imperfection. Focus on findings that affect safety, water intrusion, structure, major systems, insurability or near-term capital expense.

Decide whether your preferred remedy is a repair, seller credit, price adjustment or simply more certainty before moving forward. Your contract and professional advisors determine what options are available in your transaction.
