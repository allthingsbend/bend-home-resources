## Spring: inspect what winter revealed

- Walk the exterior for roof, siding, trim and drainage damage.
- Clean gutters and confirm downspout discharge paths.
- Look for soil or hardscape that directs water toward the home.
- Service cooling equipment before peak demand if applicable.
- Inspect decks, fences and exterior wood.
- Plan exterior paint or repair work before the busiest season.

## Early summer: prepare for heat and wildfire

- Reduce combustible debris near the structure.
- Maintain grass, vegetation and tree clearance using local fire guidance.
- Clean roof valleys and gutters where debris accumulates.
- Check irrigation for leaks and overspray against siding or foundation areas.
- Review home insurance and your household evacuation plan.

## Fall: prepare for cold weather

- Service heating equipment.
- Plan sprinkler/irrigation winterization as appropriate for your system.
- Disconnect vulnerable hoses and protect exterior plumbing.
- Clean gutters after leaf/needle drop.
- Inspect weatherstripping and exterior penetrations.
- Review roof condition before snow and freeze cycles.

## Winter: monitor rather than ignore

- Watch for ice, snow-load concerns or drainage changes around the home.
- Look for condensation or indoor moisture patterns.
- Keep vents and exterior equipment clear as appropriate.
- After severe weather, check roof, trees and exterior areas for new damage.

## Annual records to keep

Save invoices, inspection reports, permit documents, warranties, photos and dates of major maintenance. A simple home log makes future diagnosis easier and becomes valuable context when you eventually sell.
