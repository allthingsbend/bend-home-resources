## Start with the exact property, not the neighborhood reputation

Walk the street at different times. Listen for traffic and activity. Look at driveway access, slope, drainage, tree cover, neighboring uses and how the lot feels in real life.

Online maps are useful, but a home's micro-location can matter as much as the neighborhood label.

## Research the public record

Use Deschutes County DIAL for property information, assessment and sales-history research. For homes inside city limits, Bend's permit resources can help with project and permit research.

If the home has an HOA or recorded covenants, obtain the actual documents rather than relying on listing summaries.

## Evaluate the house by age and condition

The inspection plan should match the property. Older or remodeled homes may justify closer attention to electrical, plumbing, additions, roof history and permits; newer homes still need inspection and documentation review.

Ask for service history on major systems when available.

## Consider wildfire and vegetation

Bend Fire & Rescue publishes defensible-space guidance for residents. Tree cover and landscaping can be a benefit, but buyers should also think about vegetation maintenance, roof and gutter debris, access and exterior materials.

## Build your own neighborhood scorecard

- Street noise and traffic at your actual address.
- Walk/bike access that matters to your household.
- HOA rules and dues, if any.
- Lot maintenance and irrigation needs.
- Wildfire and insurance questions.
- Commute and routine destinations.
- Near-term home maintenance or renovation needs.
