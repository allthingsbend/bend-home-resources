## Break the project into four scopes

1. Source control: roof, siding, flashing, plumbing, grading or other cause.
2. Access and demolition: opening walls, removing trim, flooring or exterior materials.
3. Drying and remediation: moisture management and any specialty treatment justified by conditions.
4. Reconstruction: insulation, sheathing, drywall, siding, flooring, paint and finish work.

## Why early estimates have wide ranges

Before assemblies are opened, contractors may not know the condition of sheathing, framing or insulation. A responsible estimate may include assumptions, allowances or a range rather than pretending hidden damage is known.

Ask what evidence would move the project from the low end to the high end.

## Get the right trade first

If the source is unclear, diagnosis matters more than collecting three reconstruction bids. A roofer, plumber, siding contractor, drainage professional or general contractor may be appropriate depending on the suspected pathway.

Once the source is controlled, define the restoration scope so contractors are pricing the same outcome.

## Document everything

For a property under contract, photos, moisture readings, specialist notes and written estimates are far more useful than verbal reassurance. For an owned home, documentation can also help with future repairs and insurance conversations.
