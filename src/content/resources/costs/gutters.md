## What changes the price

- Total linear footage.
- Number of stories and roofline complexity.
- Material and profile.
- Number and placement of downspouts.
- Removal of old gutters.
- Fascia or soffit repair.
- Drainage extensions or underground connections.
- Access and site conditions.

## Scope the drainage path first

A new gutter system can still perform poorly if downspouts dump water beside the house. Decide where roof runoff should go before finalizing downspout locations.

If water has already pooled against the home, evaluate grading, hardscape slope and exterior damage as part of the same water-management problem.

## Ask bidders to separate related repairs

If fascia, soffit or siding is damaged, ask the contractor to separate those items from gutter installation. That makes bids easier to compare and helps you understand which work is essential.

## Maintenance belongs in the cost

A system that is difficult to clean or regularly clogged can create future expense. Consider roof debris, nearby trees and safe access when choosing the design.
