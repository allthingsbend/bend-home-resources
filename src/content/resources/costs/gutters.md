## Quick answer: what should you budget?

For most Bend homeowners comparing standard seamless aluminum systems, these are useful **planning ranges**, not guaranteed local quotes:

| Project or upgrade | 2026 planning range |
| --- | ---: |
| Seamless aluminum gutters, installed | **$8–$18 per linear foot** |
| Straightforward 150-foot system | **$1,200–$2,700** |
| Straightforward 200-foot system | **$1,600–$3,600** |
| Removal and disposal of old gutters | **$1–$3 per linear foot** |
| Gutter guards | **$4–$15+ per linear foot** |
| Minor fascia repair | **$300–$1,500+** |
| Complex fascia, soffit or siding repair | **Quoted separately after inspection** |
| Underground drainage or drywell work | **Often $1,000–$5,000+**, depending on scope |

National 2026 cost data for professionally installed seamless gutters spans a wide range based on home height, material and complexity. Bend pricing can differ because of labor availability, roof access, snow and ice considerations, metal choice and how runoff is managed on the property.

The most useful number is the **complete installed price**, including gutters, downspouts, outlets, elbows, extensions, hangers, removal, disposal and any drainage work. A low per-foot price is not a bargain if the quote leaves out the parts needed to move water safely away from the home.

## Estimate your Bend gutter cost

Start by estimating the gutter length around the roof edges that actually need drainage. Do not automatically use the home's square footage: a two-story 2,000-square-foot house can need less gutter than a sprawling one-story house of the same size.

Use this simple formula:

**Estimated gutter length × installed price per foot + downspout and repair allowances = preliminary budget**

Example for a relatively straightforward home:

- 180 linear feet of seamless aluminum gutters
- $8 to $18 per linear foot
- Base planning range: **$1,440 to $3,240**
- Add removal, fascia repair, guards or drainage extensions if needed

An installer should field-measure the roofline before giving a final proposal. Valleys, upper roofs, dormers and inside corners can create concentrated runoff that is not obvious from a basic perimeter measurement.

## What changes the final price?

### Home height and access

Second-story and steep-roof work generally costs more because it takes longer and requires additional safety equipment. Decks, slopes, narrow side yards and landscaping can also make setup harder.

### Roofline complexity

A long, straight ranch roof is usually less expensive than a roof with multiple corners, dormers, valleys and separate levels. More corners mean more cuts, seams, end caps and labor.

### Gutter size

Five-inch K-style gutters are common. Six-inch gutters and larger downspouts cost more, but they can provide meaningful extra capacity for large, steep or complicated roof sections. Ask why the contractor recommends a specific size.

### Material and finish

Seamless painted aluminum is the usual value choice because it is relatively light, corrosion-resistant and available in many colors. Steel is stronger but can rust if its coating is damaged. Copper is a premium architectural option and can cost several times more than aluminum. Vinyl is inexpensive but is less common for professionally installed systems and can become brittle.

### Downspouts and discharge work

The gutter only collects water. The downspouts and extensions determine where it goes. Extra downspouts, oversized outlets, long extensions, underground pipe, drywells or drainage corrections can add substantially to the project.

### Existing damage

Rotten fascia, damaged soffit, deteriorated siding and missing flashing should be addressed before or during installation. Gutters fastened to weakened wood will not stay secure. Ask contractors to price these repairs separately so you can compare bids fairly.

### Removal and disposal

Replacement projects may include labor to remove and haul away the old system. Confirm whether this is included rather than assuming it is.

## Why Bend homes need a complete drainage plan

Bend is dry compared with western Oregon, but that does not make roof drainage unimportant. Snowmelt, freeze-thaw cycles, summer storms and runoff concentrated by a large roof can still send a significant amount of water to one location.

A gutter system should protect:

- Fascia, soffits and siding
- Foundations and crawlspaces
- Entries, steps and walkways
- Decks and patios
- Sloped or erosion-prone areas
- Landscaping near the house

Before approving downspout locations, walk the property and identify where the water will end up. The discharge point should not create a new problem at the foundation, on a neighbor's property, across a walkway or on a deck.

For a larger issue involving grading, pooling or underground disposal, start with the [Bend home drainage guide](/homeownership/drainage). Gutters may be one part of the fix rather than the entire fix.

## Five-inch vs. six-inch gutters

The best gutter size depends on the amount of roof draining into each section, roof pitch, valley locations and the length between outlets.

**Five-inch gutters may be adequate when:**

- The roof design is simple
- Runs are relatively short
- Downspouts are well placed
- There are few areas of concentrated valley flow

**Six-inch gutters are worth considering when:**

- The roof is large or steep
- Upper roofs drain onto lower roofs
- Valleys send water to one short section
- Runs are long
- Pine needles or other debris are common
- The existing 5-inch system regularly overflows

Larger gutters are not a substitute for enough downspouts. Ask the bidder to explain both gutter capacity and outlet placement.

## Downspouts vs. rain chains

Rain chains can look good and make roof runoff visible, but they are not equal to downspouts in every location.

### Choose a downspout when:

- Water must be moved several feet away from the house
- The outlet is above siding, a foundation, a walkway or a deck
- Wind could blow water against the building
- The roof section carries a large volume of runoff
- You want a closed path into an extension or underground drain

### Consider a rain chain when:

- The roof section is small
- The chain ends in a properly sized basin or drain
- Splash will not reach siding or create ice on a walking surface
- The location is primarily decorative
- You are comfortable with more visible splash and possible winter ice

A rain chain ending directly on a wood deck is usually a poor choice unless a designed collection and drainage system carries the water away. It can create repeated wetting, splash, staining and ice where people walk.

If appearance is the goal, a reasonable compromise is to use rain chains in one or two safe focal locations and conventional downspouts everywhere water control matters most.

## Gutters, snow and ice in Central Oregon

Gutters do not cause ice dams, but ice and sliding snow can damage an undersized or poorly attached system. The roof's insulation, air sealing, ventilation and snow behavior all matter.

Ask the installer about:

- Hanger spacing and fastening method
- Whether the fascia is sound
- Gutter position relative to the roof edge
- Snow-shedding areas above entries and walkways
- Oversized outlets and downspouts
- Debris accumulation that can trap ice
- Whether heat cable is actually appropriate for a specific trouble spot

Do not assume heat cable should be installed everywhere. It adds operating cost and must be designed, powered and maintained correctly. If the roof develops recurring ice dams, investigate heat loss and roof ventilation rather than treating the gutter alone.

## Are gutter guards worth the cost in Bend?

Gutter guards can reduce cleaning, but no guard makes a system maintenance-free. Pine needles, small seeds, roof granules, moss and ice can still collect on top of a cover or reach the gutter.

Guards may be useful when:

- Tall trees drop debris on the roof
- Safe ladder access is difficult
- The guard is designed for the specific gutter and roof
- The installer explains how the system will still be inspected and cleaned

Before paying for guards, ask to see the exact product and its separate installed price. Also ask whether it affects the roof or gutter warranty. In some Bend settings, larger gutters and outlets plus scheduled cleaning may be a better value than an expensive cover system.

## Coordinate gutters with roofing, siding and fascia work

The order of work matters when several exterior projects overlap.

If fascia, soffit or siding is damaged, repair and paint those surfaces before the final gutter installation whenever practical. A gutter company cannot create a durable attachment to rotten fascia.

If a roof replacement is planned soon, ask both contractors:

1. Can the new roof be installed without removing the gutters?
2. If removal is necessary, who removes and reinstalls them?
3. Is that work included in the roofing bid?
4. Could removing a nearly new gutter void its warranty?
5. Should drip edge or kick-out flashing be corrected at the same time?

When a roof replacement is only a year or two away, installing gutters now can still make sense if uncontrolled water is damaging the home. The key is to coordinate the details so the system does not have to be purchased twice. See the [Bend roof replacement cost guide](/costs/roof-replacement) for related planning.

## Permits, HOA approval and contractor checks

A basic like-for-like gutter replacement often does not involve a structural permit, but related work can. Structural fascia repairs, plumbing connections, drywells, changes to a public drainage system or a larger remodel may require review. Requirements also vary between properties inside the City of Bend and addresses under Deschutes County jurisdiction.

Before work begins:

- Confirm permit requirements with the correct local building department
- Check HOA or architectural-review rules, especially for color and visible drainage features
- Verify that the contractor's Oregon CCB license is active
- Confirm liability insurance and workers' compensation where applicable
- Use a written contract that identifies materials, scope, price and warranty
- Do not rely on a verbal promise to handle concealed damage

Deschutes County also has fire-hardening requirements for covered work in applicable wildfire hazard areas. If gutters are part of a larger permitted roof or exterior project, ask whether approved noncombustible gutter and debris-management details apply to the property.

## What a complete gutter quote should include

Use the same scope for every bidder. A useful proposal should state:

- Approximate linear feet
- Gutter material, gauge, profile, size and color
- Seamless or sectional construction
- Downspout size, quantity and exact locations
- Outlet and elbow details
- Hanger type and spacing
- Removal and disposal of existing gutters
- Fascia, soffit or siding repairs
- Gutter guard product and separate price
- Extensions, splash blocks or underground connections
- Permit responsibility, if applicable
- Workmanship and material warranties
- Project schedule and payment terms
- Price for concealed wood damage if discovered

Be cautious when a proposal gives only one total and no description of the system. Our [contractor bid comparison guide](/tools/contractor-bid-comparison) can help you line up exclusions and specifications before choosing.

## Where to spend and where to save

A sensible value-focused system for many Bend homes is seamless aluminum, properly sized outlets, enough enclosed downspouts and simple above-ground extensions to safe discharge points.

Good places to spend:

- Sound fascia and secure attachment
- Correct sizing for roof valleys and long runs
- Extra or larger downspouts where runoff concentrates
- Safe discharge away from the home
- Repairs that stop ongoing water damage

Possible places to save:

- Standard aluminum instead of copper
- A common profile and color
- Guards only where debris is a demonstrated problem
- Downspouts instead of decorative rain chains in low-visibility areas
- Above-ground extensions when they work safely and look acceptable

The cheapest system is not the one with the lowest bid. It is the one that manages water reliably without creating repair costs elsewhere.

## Bottom line

For 2026 budgeting, start around **$8 to $18 per linear foot** for professionally installed seamless aluminum gutters in Bend, with many straightforward projects roughly **$1,200 to $3,600**. Then add realistic allowances for height, complex rooflines, removal, guards, fascia repair and drainage work.

Get at least two or three itemized local bids. Compare the number and placement of downspouts, how water will leave the property, the material specification and what happens if damaged wood is uncovered. Those details matter more than a single advertised price per foot.
