## Verify the contractor with Oregon CCB

Oregon's Construction Contractors Board provides tools consumers can use to check contractor licensing and recent disciplinary information. Verify the business you are actually contracting with rather than relying on a logo, referral or social profile.

For work that requires permits, also clarify who is responsible for obtaining them and how inspections will be handled.

## Know when a general contractor makes sense

A general contractor can be valuable when a project involves multiple trades, sequencing or broader responsibility for scope. A single-trade project may be better suited to a licensed specialist.

For inspection findings, sometimes the first goal is not a complete remodel bid—it is a credible estimate of what must be repaired and what uncertainty remains.

## Make quotes comparable

- Detailed scope of work.
- Materials or allowance assumptions.
- Demolition and disposal.
- Permit responsibility and fees.
- Subcontracted work.
- Site protection and cleanup.
- Payment schedule.
- Start window and expected duration.
- Change-order process.
- Warranty terms.

## Ask what is excluded

Exclusions are often where bids diverge. One quote may include paint, trim replacement and disposal while another prices only the core repair. Ask contractors to state exclusions in writing.

## Avoid paying for certainty you do not have

When hidden damage is possible, a contractor may reasonably need to give a range or contingency instead of a fixed price. Ask what would cause the price to move and what investigation could narrow the range before work starts.
