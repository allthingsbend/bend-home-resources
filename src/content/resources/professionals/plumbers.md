## When a buyer may need a plumber

- Active or recurrent leaks.
- Low pressure or inconsistent flow.
- Water-heater concerns.
- Questionable supply or drain materials.
- Improvised or visibly poor plumbing work.
- Inspection findings that need diagnosis beyond the inspector's scope.

## Ask for diagnosis before scope

For uncertain problems, ask the plumber to explain what is known, what is suspected and what additional access or testing would be needed. That is especially important when walls or floors may need to be opened.

A repair quote should distinguish the plumbing work from drywall, paint, cabinetry or other restoration that may require another trade.

## Compare written details

For larger work, compare materials, access assumptions, permit responsibility, restoration exclusions, warranty and schedule—not only hourly rates or totals.

## Watch the water-management chain

A leak may be plumbing, exterior drainage, roofing, siding or condensation. Do not force every moisture problem into the first trade you call; use evidence to identify the source.
