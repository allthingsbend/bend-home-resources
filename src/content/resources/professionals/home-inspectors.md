## Verify Oregon credentials

Oregon requires certification for individuals who inspect residential structures for a fee and provide a written report. Home inspection businesses may also have CCB requirements. Use official state resources to verify status.

Ask which inspector will personally perform the work; do not assume every person associated with a company has the same credentials or experience.

## Compare the actual inspection scope

- Roof and exterior inspection method.
- Crawlspace or attic access approach.
- Electrical, plumbing and HVAC coverage.
- Thermal imaging, moisture meters or other tools used—and their limitations.
- Whether sewer scope, radon, mold or other specialty services are separate.
- Typical report delivery time and report format.

## Ask for a sample report

A sample report shows whether the inspector explains significance and next steps or simply produces hundreds of checkboxes. Look for clear photos, location details and recommendations that distinguish monitor/maintain from urgent specialist evaluation.

## Questions worth asking before booking

1. Are you currently certified in Oregon, and how can I verify it?
2. What is included and excluded from your standard inspection?
3. What parts of the roof, attic and crawlspace do you physically access when conditions allow?
4. When do you recommend a specialist rather than giving a definitive opinion yourself?
5. Can I meet you near the end for a verbal summary?
6. How quickly will the written report be delivered?

## Price is not the only comparison

A slightly more expensive inspection can be worthwhile if the scope is stronger and the inspector communicates well. At the same time, optional add-ons are not automatically necessary. Match the inspection plan to the house.
