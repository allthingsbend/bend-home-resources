## Which type of gutter company fits your project?

### New seamless gutter installation

Start with dedicated gutter installers and compare them with an exterior contractor if other roofline work is involved. Require every bid to use the same assumed gutter size, metal, downspout count and discharge plan.

### Repairing leaks or sagging gutters

Look for a company that performs repairs rather than automatically quoting full replacement. Ask whether the problem comes from loose hangers, poor slope, failed seams, undersized outlets, ice damage or deteriorated fascia.

### Fascia, soffit or flashing damage

Choose a contractor willing to define responsibility for the damaged building materials. Some gutter companies advertise these repairs; others may require a roofer, carpenter or general contractor. Do not install a new system onto unsound fascia.

### Gutter cleaning

Cleaning alone is a different decision from construction. Oregon CCB guidance lists gutter cleaning among work that may not require a contractor license, but installation and construction-related repairs should still be checked under the applicable rules. Confirm insurance and safe-work practices for anyone working at height.

### Gutter guards and pine needles

Ask to see the exact product rather than accepting a generic line item for “guards.” Pine needles and roof grit can behave differently from broad leaves. Confirm how the guard is attached, how the gutter will still be inspected and cleaned, and whether the product affects roof or gutter warranties.

### Underground drainage

A company that installs underground pipe or drywells may be useful when downspouts cannot discharge safely near the house. This is a drainage project, not just a gutter upgrade. Ask who designs the discharge, whether utility locating or permits are needed and what happens if the soil does not accept water as expected.

## What matters specifically on a Bend home

Bend receives less annual rain than western Oregon, but a roof can still concentrate snowmelt and stormwater into a few outlets. Local projects should account for:

- Snow and ice loading
- Freeze-thaw cycles
- Steep roofs and concentrated valley runoff
- Pine needles and other roof debris
- Hillside lots and erosion
- Decks, walkways and entries below roof edges
- Wildfire-conscious debris management
- Siding and fascia exposed by missing or failed gutters

The goal is not merely to attach metal to the roof edge. The system must collect water, move it through correctly sized outlets and discharge it somewhere that does not damage the siding, foundation, deck, landscaping or neighboring property.

## How to request comparable bids

Give every company the same short written scope. Include:

- Property address
- New installation, replacement or repair
- Approximate number of stories
- Known fascia, soffit, siding or flashing damage
- Whether a roof replacement is planned
- Interest in 5-inch or 6-inch gutters
- Preferred downspout locations
- Any rain chains, guards or heat cable being considered
- Existing pooling or drainage problems
- Desired timeline

Then require the proposal to identify:

- Approximate linear footage
- Gutter profile, size, material, gauge and color
- Number and size of downspouts
- Hanger type and spacing
- Removal and disposal
- Fascia or related repair allowances
- Drainage extensions or underground connections
- Permit responsibility where applicable
- Start timing and estimated duration
- Workmanship and material warranties
- Payment schedule and exclusions

## Verify the contractor before hiring

Do not rely only on a CCB number printed on a website or estimate. Use the [official Oregon CCB license search](https://search.ccb.state.or.us/search/) immediately before hiring.

Check:

1. The legal business name matches the contract.
2. The license is active.
3. The endorsement is appropriate for the work.
4. The company information is consistent with the proposal.
5. Complaint or disciplinary information does not raise unresolved concerns.
6. Insurance and workers' compensation are current where required.

The company that answers the phone may use a trade name that differs from the legal contracting entity. Your written agreement should make the relationship clear.

## Compare recent reviews carefully

Review totals and star ratings change too often to freeze into a permanent guide. Check current Google and other credible review sources as part of your own comparison.

Look beyond the average rating for patterns involving:

- Communication after the deposit
- Accuracy of scheduling
- Care around decks, landscaping and paint
- Cleanup of sharp metal scraps and fasteners
- Response to leaks or warranty calls
- Whether downspout placement was discussed before installation
- How unexpected fascia damage was handled

A handful of detailed recent reviews can be more informative than many short ratings with no description of the work.

## Red flags in a gutter quote

Pause before hiring when:

- The business name on the bid cannot be verified
- The contractor will not provide a written scope
- The bid does not state gutter or downspout size
- A major deposit is requested without clear terms
- Fascia damage is dismissed without inspection
- Downspouts are placed without discussing discharge
- A rain chain is proposed over a deck or walkway without a collection plan
- Warranty promises are not written into the agreement
- The price is far below other bids but key work is missing

The lowest total can become the most expensive option if it excludes removal, repairs, enough downspouts or a workable drainage path.

## Our bottom line

There is no single “best gutter company in Bend” for every project. Build a shortlist based on the work you actually need, then compare at least two or three detailed proposals.

For a normal installation, focus on correct sizing, sound fascia, secure attachment and enough downspouts. For a more complicated property, prioritize a contractor who understands how roof runoff, snow, siding, grading and underground drainage work together.

The best choice is the company that provides a clear scope, verifies correctly, communicates well and proposes a system that moves water to a safe location—not simply the company with the lowest advertised price.
